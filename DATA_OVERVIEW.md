# SciNet Data Overview

SciNet maps the anatomy of scientific research across 5 domains, 34 fields, and 318 subfields, with 7,257 task statements in the public release. This page provides a visual summary of the database — task structure, research scale, verifiability, and AI adoption across disciplines.

Every task is filed at the level it genuinely belongs to: 27 apply to research everywhere, 134 to a whole domain, 321 to a field, and 6,775 to a single subfield. A subfield therefore inherits the broader tasks above it rather than restating them, which is why the website shows more task cards per page than there are tasks in the database.

Figures are generated by [`generate_figures.py`](generate_figures.py) and can be refreshed by running `python3 generate_figures.py` from the `public/` directory.

## Task Structure

**Tasks by domain** — research tasks grouped by domain. The figure uses the pipeline's six-domain grouping, which splits Mathematics, Statistics and Computer Science into a **Formal Sciences** domain; the website groups them under Physical and Social Sciences and shows five.

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lukasalthoff/scinet/main/figures/tasks_by_domain.png" alt="Tasks by domain" width="680"/></p>

**Tasks by activity category** — distribution across the eight [O\*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/)-style activity categories that structure all SciNet tasks. (These counts changed substantially in v1.3: the previous release mislabelled 89% of subfield tasks as *Ideation & Hypothesis Generation*.)

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lukasalthoff/scinet/main/figures/tasks_by_category.png" alt="Tasks by category" width="680"/></p>

**Tasks by hierarchy level** — counts at each of the four levels in the released data: universal, domain, field, and subfield. The field level is new in v1.3.

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lukasalthoff/scinet/main/figures/tasks_by_level.png" alt="Tasks by level" width="600"/></p>

**Substeps and time** — each task also ships with a substep decomposition (how the task breaks into the steps a researcher performs) and a time estimate, given separately for every subfield the task appears in. Time is split into attended effort and elapsed time, because a task can be thirty minutes of work spread across a three-week wait. See [`data/substeps.csv.gz`](data/substeps.csv.gz) and [`data/task_time.csv`](data/task_time.csv). These are model estimates, not measurements, and are not displayed on the website.

## Verifiability

SciNet computes a verifiability index for each subfield based on three components: retraction rate, hedging language frequency, and booster language frequency in paper abstracts. Higher scores indicate more cautious, verifiable research communication.

**Verifiability by domain** — distribution of subfield-level verifiability scores across the five display research domains.

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lukasalthoff/scinet/main/figures/verifiability_by_domain.png" alt="Verifiability by domain" width="680"/></p>

**Top and bottom subfields** — the 10 subfields with the highest and lowest verifiability index (among subfields with ≥ 100 papers).

<p align="center"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lukasalthoff/scinet/main/figures/verifiability_top_bottom.png" alt="Top and bottom subfields by verifiability" width="900"/></p>

## Claude Usage and AI Research Output

Claude usage data: [Anthropic Economic Index](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report), August 2025 ([dataset on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex)). Research output is restricted to the project's AI-related paper definition in [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/) (2023–2025).

**Claude usage vs. AI-related research output per capita (log scale)** — an AI-only country-level scatter that restricts OpenAlex output to the project's AI-related paper definition (2023–2025) and shows both axes on log scales. Bubble size reflects AI-related publication volume.
